Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed - AD 381

We believe in one God, the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth, and of all things visible and invisible.
And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God, begotten of the Father before all worlds; God of God, Light of Light, very God of very God; begotten, not made, being of one substance with the Father, by whom all things were made. Who, for us men and for our salvation, came down from heaven, and was incarnate by the Holy Spirit of the virgin Mary, and was made man; and was crucified also for us under Pontius Pilate; He suffered and was buried; and the third day He rose again, according to the Scriptures; and ascended into heaven, and sits on the right hand of the Father; and He shall come again, with glory, to judge the quick and the dead; whose kingdom shall have no end.
And we believe in the Holy Spirit, the Lord and Giver of Life; who proceeds from the Father and the Son;1 who with the Father and the Son together is worshipped and glorified; who spoke by the prophets; and one holy catholic and apostolic Church. We acknowledge one baptism for the remission of sins; and I look for the resurrection of the dead, and the life of the world to come. Amen.



Editorial Note:
The original Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed confessed that the Holy Spirit “proceeds from the Father.” TGN uses the traditional Western wording, **“who proceeds from the Father and the Son,”** while acknowledging that these additional words were not part of the creed’s original form in 381.
We adopt this wording chiefly because we believe it is consistent with Scripture. Jesus teaches that the Spirit proceeds from the Father and also repeatedly speaks of **sending the Spirit** to His people; Scripture likewise calls Him both **the Spirit of God** and **the Spirit of Christ** (John 15:26; 16:7; Romans 8:9; Galatians 4:6). The phrase therefore safeguards the full biblical relationship of the Holy Spirit to both the Father and the Son.
Our use of the Western wording is not based merely on later tradition, but on our conviction that it faithfully summarises the teaching of Scripture.
