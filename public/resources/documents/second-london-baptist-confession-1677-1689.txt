Editorial Note
The Second London Baptist Confession of Faith was first published anonymously in 1677 by English Particular Baptists living under religious persecution. It was publicly republished and endorsed in 1689, following greater legal toleration for Protestant dissenters, and is therefore commonly called either the 1677/1689 Confession or the 1689 Baptist Confession of Faith. 
The confession stands deliberately within the wider Protestant and Reformed tradition. Its authors drew extensively from the Westminster Confession of Faith and the Savoy Declaration, often retaining their language to demonstrate substantial agreement with Presbyterians and Congregationalists concerning the foundational doctrines of Christianity. At the same time, they revised certain sections to express distinctly Baptist convictions concerning the biblical covenants, the constitution and government of local churches, and the baptism of professing believers. 
Its thirty-two chapters offer a comprehensive summary of Christian doctrine, including Scripture, the Triune God, creation, providence, the fall, God’s covenant, Christ the Mediator, effectual calling, justification, sanctification, perseverance, worship, the Church, baptism, the Lord’s Supper, and the final judgment. Particularly valuable is its insistence that Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of saving knowledge, faith, and obedience, and that Christ gathers His people into visible local churches ordered according to His Word. 
TGN publishes this confession because it represents one of the clearest and most mature expressions of historic Reformed Baptist theology. Its emphasis on sovereign grace, the sufficiency of Christ, meaningful church membership, qualified leadership, church discipline, believer’s baptism, and the ordinary means of grace remains deeply relevant to African churches confronting doctrinal shallowness, individualistic Christianity, prosperity teaching, unaccountable leadership, and confusion concerning the nature of the local church.
TGN includes both Reformed Baptist and Presbyterian contributors. Publishing this confession therefore does not imply that every contributor affirms its distinctively Baptist conclusions, particularly concerning covenant theology, the proper subjects of baptism, and aspects of church government. It is presented as an important historic confessional witness that substantially reflects TGN’s Protestant and Reformed convictions while representing more specifically the Baptist tradition of many within the platform.
The confession is not inspired or infallible and possesses no authority independently of God’s Word. TGN receives and commends it only insofar as it faithfully summarises the teaching of Holy Scripture, which alone remains our final and infallible authority.
